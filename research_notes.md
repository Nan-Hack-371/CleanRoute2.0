# CleanRoute Bengaluru Pilot — Research Notes

## Scope and method

This is a desk-research pilot for five high-footfall toilet locations in Bengaluru. The user-requested ratings for cleanliness, water availability, and safety cannot be represented as on-site inspections or commuter testimony without fieldwork. The app will therefore identify its location-card scores as **indicative desk-research priority scores**—for comparison and pilot testing only—and will invite commuter verification.

## Verified citywide context

The *Bengaluru Public Toilet Audit Report 2026*, as reported by *The Indian Express*, surveyed 142 toilets in Greater Bengaluru Authority limits during April–May 2026. It assessed functionality, hygiene, accessibility, safety, and user experience. The report found that 37 facilities (26%) were defunct; only six (4.2%) were rated very clean; 58% had persistent foul odour; lighting was adequate at 56%; water was available at about 40% of functioning toilets; and nine locations had ramps. These data frame the need for cautious, transparent facility discovery rather than guarantee that any individual toilet is currently usable. [1]

## Metro-facility evidence

*The Hindu* reported in May 2025 that BMRCL had placed toilets in the **unpaid areas** of 12 stations under Sulabh International management. The first-phase list included National College, Lalbagh, South End Circle, Jayanagar, Rashtriya Vidyalaya Road, Banashankari, Jaya Prakash Nagar, Yelachenahalli, Sir M Visvesvaraya Station (Central College), Dr BR Ambedkar Station (Vidhana Soudha), Cubbon Park, and Krantivira Sangolli Rayanna Railway Station. The article reported an initial ₹2 urinal / ₹5 toilet charge. Four days later, *The Indian Express* reported that BMRCL withdrew the fees and ended the Sulabh contract after commuter protests, while stating it would directly maintain the toilets. It also confirmed that paid-zone toilets had been free to Metro passengers and that the withdrawn fees applied only outside fare control. [2] [3]

## Direct map-listing evidence

Google Maps search results on 19 August 2026 showed several public-toilet listings in the Cubbon Park / Cubbon Park Metro vicinity. These included “Cubbon Park Toilet” (2.7/5 from 20 user ratings; listed hours 07:30–18:30), “Public Toilet (Inside Cubbon Park)” (2.1/5 from 19 ratings), and “Public Toilet” at Cubbon Park Road / Ambedkar Veedhi (2.4/5 from 11 ratings). A search around National College Metro surfaced “National Bathroom” at plus code 3F58+8PQ, rated 3.7/5 from 15 ratings and listed as open until 20:00. Map scores describe general user feedback, not standalone hygiene, water, or safety findings. [4] [5]

The Jayanagar Metro search returned a dense set of publicly listed facilities. The selected “Pay & Use Toilet, Jayanagar” at WH9R+HPC, 22nd Main Road held a 3.4/5 rating from 84 user ratings and was listed as open until 21:00. An “SBM Toilet” explicitly located at Jayanagar 4th Block bus station was listed as open 24 hours and rated 4.1/5 from seven ratings. In the Majestic hub, the selected “Pay & use toilet” on Gubbi Thotadappa Road was listed as open 24 hours with a 3.2/5 score from 33 ratings; other nearby listings showed substantial variation, including a 1.3/5 facility on Parcel Office Road and a 4.4/5 facility on Tank Bund Road. This variation reinforces the value of comparing specific listings rather than assuming the station-area average. [6] [7]

For KR Market, the selected “Pay And Use Public Toilet” at plus code XH8G+F8H was listed as open 24 hours with a 2.9/5 score from seven ratings. Map results also showed an “SBM Toilet” at the KR Market Indus Canteen / SJPR Road bus-stand area, further confirming the hub contains multiple public facility listings. [8]

## Rating framework for this pilot

Janaagraha’s 2023 Public Toilet Quality Survey assessed 22 Bengaluru parks across 18 indicators under four broad parameters: accessibility; comfort and safety; cleanliness and maintenance; and complaints/grievance redressal. CleanRoute adopts this separation: a general map-listing score is displayed only as a **crowd-feedback signal**, while cleanliness, water, safety, women-friendly, and wheelchair-accessible status remain **unverified** until a participant completes a structured field check. This prevents a general user score from being falsely presented as proof of water availability, accessibility, or safety. [9]

## Maharashtra expansion — preliminary map listing evidence

Google Maps results accessed on 19 August 2026 returned multiple SBM Toilet listings across Khopoli. The first listed SBM Toilet was shown on Mohana Roky Road with a 5.0/5 score from one public rating and 24-hour listed availability. Other nearby SBM listings were also shown as 24-hour facilities, but the listing names and rating sample sizes were too repetitive and small to support a site-condition claim. The Khopoli record will therefore be labelled as a low-sample discovery listing, not a hygiene or accessibility rating. [10]

For Rasayani, Google Maps returned “Rasayani Corner Toilet” at V5R6+JFF, Sawala Apta Road, with a 5.0/5 score from one public rating, alongside a separate “Public Toilet” at V5R7+RMX, Dand Apta Road with no visible reviews. The very small evidence base means both water availability and safety/access conditions remain unverified. [11]

## Maharashtra-first redesign requirements

The attached college-project brief requires Maharashtra—not Bengaluru—to be the primary study area. The app must center Khopoli, Rasayani, Panvel and Navi Mumbai; preserve the existing editorial civic-tech visual system; use a single data source to drive cards, filters, map markers, evidence cards and statistics; and distinguish public listings from on-site evidence. The requested status vocabulary includes *Location sourced from public listing*, *Needs field check*, *Community reported*, *Verified*, and *Not rated*. No condition, access, or hygiene claim should be presented as personal fieldwork without a corresponding observation.
